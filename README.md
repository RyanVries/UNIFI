## 🍽️ In Lieu of a Methods Section: A Recipe

*Since we are not giving anything away, here is the only protocol we are prepared to share publicly.*

### Slow-Release Hexamer Lasagna
*Serves: 6 (it has to be 6) | Prep time: 30 min | Onset of action: ~45 min | Duration: until the next day's leftovers*

**Ingredients**
- 6 lasagna sheets *(exactly six; this is non-negotiable)*
- 500 g minced meat or lentils *(both formulations have been validated)*
- 1 jar of tomato sauce
- 250 ml béchamel sauce *(acts as the protamine: delays release, adds stability)*
- 150 g grated cheese *(binds everything together, much like albumin)*
- 1 onion and 2 cloves of garlic, finely chopped
- Salt and pepper, to taste *(titrate individually)*
- Zinc: none. We checked. Your lasagna is not a pharmaceutical formulation.

**Instructions**

1. Preheat the oven to 180 °C. Fry the onion, garlic, and mince until browned. Add the tomato sauce and simmer for 15 minutes.
2. Assemble the hexamer: alternate sauce and sheets until all six sheets are used. Any fewer and it dissociates too quickly; any more and it becomes ultra-long-acting.
3. Cover with béchamel and cheese. This depot must remain stable at the site of administration (the table) until serving.
4. Bake for 40 minutes. Absorption from the oven into the kitchen will be slow at first, then rapid; this is normal biphasic behavior.
5. Let it rest for 10 minutes before cutting. Premature serving leads to structural collapse and poor identifiability of the individual layers.
6. Serve. Note that the dissociation rate of a lasagna is inversely proportional to the hunger of those present.

*Note: Each slice is absorbed at roughly 1/√6 the rate of a single layer eaten on its own. We did not test this experimentally. Reviewers, please do not ask for this experiment.*
